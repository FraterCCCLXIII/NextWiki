THE SCIENCE / THE ASSUMPTIONS

What the window shows.

This is an instantaneous inertial sky observation, not a spacecraft engineering model. The background is seeded synthetic directions at infinity. A separate finite catalog of 700 synthetic stationary stars and six approximate catalog locations translates relative to the craft along the selected straight-line path. Its directions use the same relativistic aberration and Doppler factors. Stationary sources retain their positions at retarded emission time, so the received ray points along the source-to-observer separation at observation. The scene excludes stellar proper motion and gravity. Dragging rotates your gaze, not your velocity. No cinematic star trails are added in relativity mode. Mission mode starts and ends at rest, with bounded proper acceleration and a selected speed cap. Legacy coast mode begins at the selected speed and treats changes as instantaneous. Earth coordinate time advances at the chosen multiplier. Mission arrival is at a staging point, not a planetary landing or capture. Navigation diamonds and projected distance rings are instructional overlays, with exaggerated symbol sizes and no physical luminosity. Nearby stellar symbols use approximate inverse-square brightness and Doppler-shifted blackbody colors; they are not calibrated telescope images.

Relativistic optics

For a rest-frame source direction μ = cos θ measured from the forward velocity axis:

μ′ = (μ + β) / (1 + βμ)
D = γ(1 + βμ) = 1 / [γ(1 − βμ′)]
ν′ = Dν    T′ = DT    γ = cosh η
β = tanh η    1 − β = 2 / (exp(2η) + 1)

The renderer inversely maps each camera ray to the stationary sky. A 24,000-star catalog is splatted into a sky texture; each star has a 2,800–12,000 K blackbody spectrum. Colors use three visible wavelength samples (610, 550, 460 nm), an educational approximation rather than calibrated CIE human vision. In radiance mode, Planck Bλ at DT implements the blackbody spectral transformation; integrated radiance scales as D⁴. Pixel filtering and tone mapping limit stellar photometric accuracy.

The CMB is a uniform 2.7255 K blackbody transformed in each viewing direction. It can enter visible wavelengths at extreme γ; there is no sharp visibility threshold. Normalized mode rescales each spectrum, while radiance mode preserves approximate relative radiance before display exposure and tone mapping. Neither models your eye's full adaptation.

Reading the supplied transcript critically

Constant 1g refers to proper acceleration. Coordinate acceleration decreases as a/γ³. A symmetric 4.4 ly trip gives roughly 3.6 crew years and 6.0 Earth years. Statements about stars becoming exactly 10,000 times brighter need a specified distance, spectrum, and measured brightness quantity; γ alone does not imply that number. At γ = 250 million, 1 − β ≈ 8 × 10⁻¹⁸, not 2 × 10⁻⁹.

Beyond the light limit

A massive observer has no rest frame at c or beyond it in special relativity. Warp mode is an artistic tunnel with an assumed constant or smooth effective-speed envelope, not a prediction of Alcubierre optics. Smooth warp uses β_eff = β_peak sin²(πt/T) with T = 2L/β_peak in years and light-years. No physical crew time or energy is inferred. Auto playback normalizes the entire route to 45 wall-clock seconds; it does not change physical travel time. A warp metric, worldline and ray tracing would be needed to predict its view. Controllable FTL combined with Lorentz symmetry can allow causal loops; this demo does not simulate them.

THE CONSTANT-ACCELERATION SHIP

Go far. Age differently.

Start and finish at rest. Accelerate halfway, then decelerate halfway at constant proper acceleration. Idealized flat spacetime; unlimited propulsion and shielding assumed.

For one way: τ = 2c/a acosh(1 + aL/2c²); t = 2c/a sinh(aτ/2c). Return doubles both times. No cruise or stay. Cosmological expansion excluded; galaxy-scale results are approximations, not predictions of future destinations.

MISSION ENGINEERING / IDEALIZED RELATIVITY

Plan the burns. Pay the cost.

Analytic acceleration, optional coast, and braking to rest on every leg. A constant operating mass and external force are assumed. This gives useful mechanical requirements, not a demonstrated engine or complete fuel budget.

Six static star systems are screened. Keep-out radii are your assumptions, not catalog-derived safe distances. Preset star centers become staging points just outside their zone. Custom endpoints inside a zone are rejected. The test zone is fictional and deliberately enlarged to make a detour visible.

Collision and sensing assumptions

Sensing numbers are ideal constant-speed causal limits, not demonstrated detection performance. Passive range is separation at detection; active range is separation at pulse transmission. Lateral time uses a local low-speed displacement approximation. Grain energy is for one spherical 3,000 kg/m³ grain. Gas is a uniform hydrogen scenario. No dust size distribution, collision probability, shield survival, or safe-route claim is inferred.

Equations, limits, and research sources

Constant proper acceleration a uses γ = √(1 + (at/c)²), x = (c²/a)(γ − 1), τ = (c/a)asinh(at/c). The speed cap creates a coast phase if there is enough distance. Each avoidance waypoint is a full rest stop; turns and engine slews have zero modeled duration. A force step is an idealization, not a jerk-limited controller.

F = ma; a_coordinate = a/γ³. K = (γ − 1)mc². Supplied-energy lower bound = ΣK_peak/efficiency. Braking must remove ΣK_peak, separately; no recovery credit or powered-braking fuel is modeled. Input power = Fv/efficiency in the external inertial frame. Exhaust energy, carried propellant, beam losses, radiator mass and engine mass are excluded. Ideal rocket initial/final mass ratio = exp(Σ|Δη| / (v_exhaust/c)); this is a diagnostic for the same rapidity changes, not the mass model used in the simulation.

Candidate waypoints around inflated spheres form a visibility graph, with a shortest sampled path search. Every segment is checked, but this is neither a globally optimal dynamical trajectory nor an actual mission clearance. Static positions neglect motion and uncertainty. Real planning needs ephemerides, covariance propagation, planetary departure/capture, gravity and low-thrust optimization. Cosmological expansion is excluded.

At constant speed, proper lateral acceleration α requires R ≥ γ²β²c²/α. Ideal crew warning time is R/(γβc) for passive detection at current separation; active echo warning from transmission range R is R(1−β)/[γβc(1+β)]. Gas swept mass = n m_H A L; single-grain impact energy = (γ−1)m_grain c².
