MODEL ENGINE / JAVASCRIPT

Write your own universe.

Function body, evaluated per region. Inputs: region, params, helpers. Return probabilities per represented star: {habitable, life, intelligence, active, signal}, each between 0 and 1. Region fields: ageGyr, metallicity, hazard, distanceLy, stars, id. Scripts cannot access the page or network. 5-second run limit.

A map of assumptions.

Dynamics heat map: one or twelve conditional seeded histories of the user-specified origin count, not a posterior estimate or confidence interval. Births are uniform across the timeline, with spatial sampling weighted by the intelligence script. Cluster mode further weights four randomly selected synthetic hotspots. Rare intelligence retains each assumed origin with 1% probability; it is not independent evidence. The dynamics lifetime is separate from the original probability-model lifetime.

Effective front speed = 10 ly / (10 ly / ship speed + settlement delay). Reached fractions approximate represented stars by testing 6,000 region centers within Euclidean spheres; this coarse sampling cannot resolve individual systems or small fronts. With expansion disabled, only the individual origin hosts count toward reach, with optional deterministic habitat selection; overlaps are counted once for total reach. Fronts stop when their origin dies; persistent settlements retain reached territory but do not independently continue expanding. The trailing window includes origins alive at any point in that window. In ensemble mode, heat values are the fraction of 12 histories reaching each represented region (or overlapping there), conditional on the same four hotspots. This small sample is illustrative and does not provide calibrated uncertainty. The rings show only the first history. Screen projection blends nearby regions, so visual brightness is not a precise probability readout. Origin density is Gaussian-smoothed; smoothing does not increase the computed reached fraction. Rings illustrate disk cross-sections of the spherical fronts. This simplified model omits star motions, hopping networks, cosmological expansion and observational selection. It does not infer civilizations from our lack of detections.

Research context: Carroll-Nellenback et al. · galactic settlement and the Aurora effect. No empirical calibration is claimed.

This is a seeded procedural universe, not an observational catalog. Locations, ages, metallicities and hazard values are synthetic. Changing scale generates a different population; views are not nested surveys. All life and civilization probabilities are speculative conditional model outputs.

Default model: habitable = planet fraction × habitable fraction × metallicity × survival. Life = habitable × life emergence × age eligibility. Intelligence = life × intelligence fraction. Active = intelligence × technological fraction × min(lifetime / eligible time window, 1). Assumes one technological onset uniformly distributed in the eligible window, with independent systems and Poisson population counts. No colonization or correlated emergence.

“Now” is a hypothetical common cosmic-time slice. Signal overlap evaluates the same active-window approximation at emission time (age minus light travel time); it is not telescope detectability. It excludes flux, beaming, receiver sensitivity and duty cycle. Distances are illustrative Euclidean light-years, not a cosmological distance calculation.

Each point aggregates many stars. Colors show log-scaled per-star probabilities, normalized within the current layer. Expected counts sum stars × probability; chance ≥1 is 1 − exp(−expected). These are model expectations, not evidence. Zero is preserved.
