NUMERICAL FLIGHT LAB

From estimates to simulated flight

3-DOF · EXPERIMENTAL

These experiments integrate separate flight segments. They do not yet form a continuous, optimized pad-to-surface mission. A successful run passes your entered limits in this model; it does not establish flight readiness.

Atmosphere, sensors, entry systems & faults
Touchdown, structural and thermal constraints

Starting state is a local descent/entry scenario, not the main planner’s interplanetary arrival. Target rotates during flight. Terrain uses a spherical datum plus entered elevation; slope is a touchdown screening value, not a terrain mesh. Chute opening shock, atmospheric chemistry and landing-gear impact forces are not solved.

Earth ascent experiment

Uses your Launch stack stages and delivered LEO mass. Point-mass propagation includes rotating Earth/air, exponential density, drag, finite thrust and fuel depletion, staging, max-Q throttling and a rate-limited pitch schedule plus terminal altitude/velocity feedback. Attitude torques and aero stability are not solved.

The schedule search tests 12 candidates against target perigee/apogee. It is a coarse search, not a globally optimal trajectory. Launch corridors, range safety and abort landing sites are not evaluated.

Dated planetary transfer

A separate 3D, solar two-body Lambert solver. Built-in dated positions use JPL’s approximate 1800–2050 elements (Earth–Moon barycenter as an Earth proxy), not Horizons ephemerides. UTC dates are treated approximately as dynamical dates; leap seconds / TDB conversion are not implemented.

The finite-burn experiment applies heliocentric Δv starting at the departure-body center, outside its gravity model. It measures error against the ideal Lambert path. It is not a departure from a parking orbit, and it is not connected to the ascent or descent state. Arrival capture is not propagated here. Imported states must share heliocentric ECLIPJ2000 axes, TDB epochs, meters and m/s.

Monte Carlo landing ensemble

Uses the current surface-landing inputs. Independent Gaussian thrust, density, initial-position and wind variations are applied to each run. Navigation biases are seeded separately. The success fraction is conditional on this model and these distributions.

Reference telemetry comparison

Import independent reference JSON samples {t, altitude, speed} to compare with the latest ascent or landing trace using linear time interpolation. RMSE is a numerical comparison, not hardware-in-the-loop qualification. Data is kept in this browser session.
