Spacecraft configuration

One spacecraft carries all mission propellant from LEO. Spacecraft staging, surface refueling, and discarded hardware are not assumed.

Atmospheric credit reduces the descent allowance only. It does not simulate entry, size a heat shield, or guarantee survivability. Dry mass must already include tanks, engines, structure, power, thermal, avionics and landing hardware.

Concept planner assumptions

These assumptions describe the original mission-sizing planner. The Flight dynamics lab adds independent numerical experiments with their own inputs, sources and limits.

Concept-level mass and velocity sizing for Earth launch, idealized outbound transfer, destination braking, descent, ascent, and an optional aligned return. Results are estimates, not flight plans or vehicle certification.

Trajectory

Planet and small-body orbits are circular, coplanar, at rounded semimajor axes. Initial phases are synthetic. Model day zero is not a calendar epoch. Hohmann paths meet the modeled destination at arrival; return windows are solved separately. A custom date cannot be converted to a real launch window with this model.

The Moon uses an Earth-centered transfer with an aligned departure in a circular lunar orbit. Other moons use a parent-system intercept plus an editable Δv allowance; their actual satellite encounter is not solved. Libration destinations use allowances only, with no trajectory animation. Eccentric/retrograde objects need a higher-fidelity solution.

Mass and engines

For each burn: m_before = m_after × exp(Δv / (Isp × g₀)). Propellant is their difference. Burn duration uses mass flow = thrust / (Isp × g₀). Earth-orbit ascent assumes direct launch into the requested plane when accessible from the launch latitude; otherwise a separate inclination change is charged at apogee. Stage Δv uses wet mass including all upper stages and spacecraft. Burn margins are applied before sizing. Crew supplies = crew × kg/day × total elapsed mission days including ground wait (conservative carried allocation).

“Basic mass checks pass” only covers tanks, ascent Δv, liftoff TWR and fairing. It is not a declaration of mission feasibility. Low-thrust, nuclear and fusion presets do not provide a validated trajectory. Starship-like values are illustrative inputs, not current SpaceX specifications.

Arrival and return

Patched-conic capture uses the estimated hyperbolic excess speed and a low parking orbit. Surface descent and ascent use circular speed plus fixed loss allowances; atmospheric braking is an adjustable assumption. No terrain, aerodynamics, parachute sizing, heat flux, atmospheric density profile, ascent steering, radiation or weather is simulated. Giant planets and the Sun cannot be surface landing destinations.

Return sizing carries the return fuel from Earth. Surface fuel production is not assumed. Optional LEO refueling launches the dry craft and carried supplies; tanker logistics are excluded. Earth entry assumes a qualified entry system; the terminal powered burn is not a substitute for reentry physics. Probe impact entry and flyby free-return paths are not solved.
