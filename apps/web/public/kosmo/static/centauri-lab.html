Centauri Fleet

Eight payloads, 4.37 light years, rebuilt from parts: real modules, buses, arrays, radiators and interfaces, a sail sized by its areal density, and a driver sized by photon momentum. Everything below is measured from those parts, not typed in from a concept table.
—

1 · What the parts weigh

The class is an assembly, not a shape. Each row below is a real part or a stated derived law; the mass is their sum, and the mates are solved from ports, so moving one part moves everything above it.

2 · The sail, and why size is not payload

The sail dominates the geometry. Its diameter is 2 √(m / (π σ)), so it grows as the square root of mass while the drawn hull grows hardly at all. Below the log ruler, the module or capsule is a few metres, the sail is kilometres, and the collector for the driver is larger again.

3 · The driver: photon momentum, not energy

A laser sail gets F = 2Pη / (c(1+β)): at rest two photon momenta per unit energy, and less as it runs away from the beam. Time to a target speed therefore fixes the power, and the table's energy column does not fix it at all. That is why the measured peak laser is several times the published figure for the same boost.

4 · The station, and its orbit

The station has to make the laser power, radiate what it does not emit, and put the beam on a sail that is receding. Collector mass falls as the square of the solar distance, but the radiator is set by laser power and does not care where it is, and the aperture is set by range and sail size. The trade below scans solar distance and aperture and reports the lightest feasible station — that is what “optimal orbit” means here.

    Sources and assumptions

    Parts and their figures carry a source in static/parts/parts.js: Columbus (NASA), Kibo (JAXA), the ISS SAW array (NASA, 31 kW / 1,090 kg), the PVR radiator (NASA CR-19940020352, 7.5 kW / 93 m²), RUAG PAF ratings, IDSS and CBM interfaces. The sail law is Lubin's A Roadmap to Interstellar Flight (sail mass equal to payload, 100 nm silicon nitride). Station assumptions are named constants in static/parts/beam.js: 50 % wall-plug laser, 30 % PV, 1 kg/m² collector, 8.8 kg/m² of radiator rejecting 4.2 kW/m² at 450 K, 5 kg/m² of phased-array aperture, a 100 km aperture cap and a 1,000 km² structure cap. The source table is SpaceRadio's centauri-fleet/fleet.mjs, reproduced unchanged in static/parts/fleet.js so the comparison cannot drift.
