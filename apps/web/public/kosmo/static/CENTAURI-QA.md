# Centauri QA pass -- every ship, every version, checked and re-fitted

The delivered ships had rings through hulls, wings through living space, parts inside each other and
parts floating free, and no human-scale detail to give the eye a sense of size. This pass audited all
32 ships (8 v1 sail classes, 3 v2 ring variants, 8 v3 driven ships, 8 v4 realism-pass ships, 5 v5
robotic designs) with an automated geometry audit and a visual review, fixed every defect at its cause,
added a library of real-size fittings and a livery library, and pinned the checks so none of it can
come back.

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| The audit (pure geometry on the solver output) | `parts/geometry-audit.js` |
| The checks (audit + headless three.js mesh audit + detail and livery minima) | `centauri-geometry-audit.test.mjs` (2,000+ checks) |
| Documented re-cuts of pinned numbers | `parts/fleet-recuts.js`, generated by `centauri-recut.mjs` |
| Human-scale fittings | `parts/primitives/detail.js` (specs and builders), `parts/detail-pass.js` (placement from data) |
| Livery | `parts/primitives/livery.js` (palette, eight liveries, procedural painters) |
| Before renders (as delivered) | `centauri-shots/*.png` (v4) and `centauri-shots/robotic/*.png` (v5) |
| After renders | `centauri-shots/qa/<version>/<ship>-<view>.png`, `centauri-shots/livery/<ship>-<view>.png` |
| Screenshot automation | `centauri-lab.html?shots=...`, `centauri-robotic.html?shots=...`, `centauri-shots-receiver.mjs` |

## 1. The audit

`auditFleet()` reads every ship's solved assembly and builds analytic bodies: a cylinder with end cones
for every hull segment, a TRUE torus for every ring (a box around a 112 m ring is all hole), swept
annuli for the bola (pods and spokes sweep as it turns), the drive by its components (frame, store,
plate, radiator panels), and an oriented box for every panel of every wing at five fold fractions
(0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) and four sun-tracking rolls (0, 45, 90, 135 degrees). Then:

| Check | Code | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Interpenetration | `interpenetration`, `mated-overlap` | sampled surface of A pushed through the signed distance of B: unmated pairs may not share volume; mated pairs may seat but not nest |
| Deployment sweep | `deployable-sweep` | no wing panel, at any fold or roll, crosses a pressurised body or a ring's swept torus |
| Rings | `ring-tilt`, `ring-offset`, `ring-clearance` | every torus coaxial with the thrust axis and centred on it; everything in its plane clears the inner wall by max(1 m, 2 % R) or sits clear outside the outer wall |
| Mates | `mate-gap`, `mate-normal`, `hardpoint-buried`, `hardpoint-floating` | the two ports of every mate coincide with opposed normals; every hardpoint stands on its part's surface |
| Floating | `floating`, `solver-disconnected` | every body has a mate path and seats against its parent |
| Scale | `giant-slab`, `module-too-thin`, `module-too-fat`, `ring-tube-thin` | no deployable wider and longer than anything flown; a person can stand in every pressurised body; every crewed ring tube holds a deck |

Baseline (before any fix): **1,471 errors, 21 warnings** across the 32 ships. After: **0 errors, 3
warnings** (the v1 seed's 21.4 m cargo can, pinned as published and listed in CENTAURI-AUDIT.md F10).

## 4. Human-scale detail

`DETAIL` in `parts/primitives/detail.js` states every size once with its source: M10 hex heads (17 mm AF),
6 mm rivets at 150 mm pitch, 38 mm handrails at 55 mm standoff in 0.6 and 1.2 m lengths, ladders at
280 mm rung pitch with cage hoops above 2.2 m, foot-restraint plates, 60 mm tether D-rings, 30 mm cable
runs with P-clamps every 0.6 m, hatches (CBM 1.27 m square, IDSS 0.8 m, Dragon 0.8 x 1.0 m, Quest 1.0 m),
windows (Cupola 0.8 m, ISS 0.51 m, Dragon 0.3 m) with frames, bolt circles and physical glazing
(`MeshPhysicalMaterial` with transmission), access panels, vents, fill ports, nameplates, docking targets,
MMOD patches. Bolt circles carry the connector's real count (CBM 16, IDSS 12, EELV 1575 120).

`parts/detail-pass.js` places them from the part KIND on the real surfaces (`cylSurface(theta, y)`,
`torusSurface(phi, psi)`): four handrail rows every metre along a pressurised barrel with tether rings
and circumferential rails at the ends, rivet rows on the 1.6 m panel bays and stringers between them, a
bolt circle on every structural port, a framed hatch with hinges, handle and docking target at every open
crew berth, two ISS windows per crewed module, access panels, vents, MMOD patches, a cable run beside the
raceway, a nameplate stencilled with the ship's registration; fill ports, rivet seams and handrails on
tanks; rails round a ring's equator at 1.2 m with hatches and flanges at the spoke heads; a caged ladder
from the hatch to the foot of a lander; servicing fittings (bolts, fiducials, a tether ring, a nameplate)
on probes. Everything is instanced: a world ship's six thousand fittings are under sixty draw calls.
The viewers show it as the "detail" layer (checkbox in the lab, HUD button in v5).

## 5. Livery

`parts/primitives/livery.js`: a palette (signal red, orange, sunflower, cobalt, mint, teal against warm
white, black, grey), eight class liveries (StarChip, Swift, Lantern, Kestrel, Pelican, Meridian, Harrow,
Ark Royal) with a primary, secondary, accent, trim, a checker pair, a hazard pair, a registration and
the pattern each part kind wears, and the painters: checker, checker band, colour blocks with tape-stripe
edges and stencils, chevron bands, hazard stripes, diagonal stripes, the rocket roll pattern, solid.
Textures are painted per mesh at the mesh's own cell grid -- a cylinder of radius r gets round(2 pi r / cell)
cells round and round(h / cell) along, a cone's cells narrow with its radius, a torus gets its ring and
tube counts -- so the squares are square, with per-cell tint variation, dirt and streaks in the colour map,
wear in the roughness map and the cell seams in the normal map. Thermal and optical surfaces (radiators,
cells, MLI, TPS, film, glazing, bells, chrome, gold, dishes, reactors) are never painted. Every version
of a class wears the same livery and the robots wear their mothership's. Viewers: "livery" layer toggle
and a "Livery (painted PBR)" render mode. Renders: `centauri-shots/livery/`.

## 6. Visual review

Renders are in `centauri-shots/qa/<version>/` (overview, plan, side, close-up, section, ring for v1-v4;
overview, hangar, ring, deck, drive for v5) and `centauri-shots/livery/`. The delivered renders in
`centauri-shots/*.png` and `centauri-shots/robotic/*.png` are the "before". Findings from the review that
the audit could not see, and what was done: seam hoops from the rivet pass (defect 17), spokes too thin
(14), nameplate lettering mirrored (texture flipped), v5 shell bands too dense (capped at six), stencils
too large on small bodies (capped at half a cell and a twelfth of the way round), hatch doors painted
(hatches are bare metal), painted end cones under two cells (solid primary with trim), MLI shell on the
v5 bays (kept: it is MLI).

## 7. Open items

- The v1 seed's 21.4 m cargo can on a 2 m berthing ring is pinned as published (CENTAURI-AUDIT.md F10);
  the audit lists it as a warning, not an error.
- Livery textures are painted in the browser (canvas); in node the painted material falls back to the
  livery's primary colour, so the headless checks verify the plan (which surfaces, which pattern, which
  cell grid), not the pixels.
- The v3 drives' radiator panels are drawn and audited from `parts/propulsion.js`'s plan but are not
  separate parts, so they have no mass of their own beyond the plan's figure.
