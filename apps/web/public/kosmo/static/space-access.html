Space Access

The lightest, cheapest way to orbit, the Moon and Mars — and what to fly there. First-order math; every constant is tagged in space-access.js. To fly a design, open the Shipyard.

1 · The cost of getting up

Orbit is mostly sideways: ~7.8 km/s along the ground, versus a few hundred m/s of altitude. The budget is Δv = vcirc − rotation + gravity + drag + steering. Gravity loss grows as thrust-to-weight falls (long burns), which is the price of cheap vacuum-only engines.

Architectures to Earth orbit

“Gross / payload” is the lightness measure: kilograms on the pad per kilogram in orbit. Cost uses order-of-magnitude hardware, propellant and ops figures; reused hardware is amortised over its flight count. A dash means the rocket equation has no solution.

2 · Lifting the rocket first

A zero-pressure balloon floats where buoyancy equals load plus its own skin: lift = ρ(h)(1 − Mgas/Mair) per m³. Air thins by ~10× every 16 km, so the volume needed explodes with altitude while a thin film's weight only grows with area.

The biggest balloons ever flown are ~1.7 million m³ and lift a few tonnes at 40 km. The default 24 t load is a ~1 t-to-orbit rocket. Heavier vehicles need a stage that already works at low T/W.

3 · Moon and Mars

Past LEO the delta-v is small and the winner is whatever avoids carrying propellant: aerocapture, depots, and propellant made on arrival.

5 · Roadmap

  • Balloon and aircraft launches fly in the Shipyard (set a release altitude and speed). Next: model the carrier, ascent drift and the balloon’s own mass in the sim, and use flown results to recalibrate the formula here.
  • Not yet modelled: kinetic launchers, skyhooks and tethers, nuclear-thermal and electric stages, bi-elliptic and low-energy transfers, radiation dose, launch windows, a real cost curve.
  • Colony and habitat economics need an in-situ construction model (regolith shielding, lunar and asteroid mass) — the torus mass is dominated by shielding.
