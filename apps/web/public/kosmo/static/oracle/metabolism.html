Food, fresh water, the reach of the health system and the traffic that carries a pathogen

World Development Indicators from the World Bank, 1960 to the latest year. Food and population are indexed to 1961 so the two can share an axis; the levels themselves are not comparable and are not shown.

Why this page exists. Every other page here is about what production becomes. This one is about the throughput that has to keep working for there to be anybody to produce for: calories, fresh water, a health system that reaches the people who need it, and the air traffic that moves a novel pathogen to every continent inside a week. A civilisation fails through this layer first and usually without warning — not because the machines stopped, but because the distribution did.

What is taken, against what is actually there

The charts above are all indexed — shapes without magnitudes. This section is in cubic kilometres and megatonnes, because a share tells you how close to the edge something is and a volume tells you how much is being moved to get there. Both are flows measured against flows: the water a country's territory renews each year, the calories its people need each day.
