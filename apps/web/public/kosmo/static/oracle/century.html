Levers

Calibrated to measured trends where they exist — Epoch AI on compute, UN WPP on population, Acemoglu–Restrepo on task shares. Where no measurement exists the value is a judgement, and the levers say so.

Read this before the numbers. Point forecasts beyond about five years carry little information: Tetlock's expert-forecasting study found accuracy decaying toward chance by five years, and there is no scored, resolved dataset at 10–25 years. Nothing here is a prediction for 2126. What the model produces is a distribution of paths and an attribution of what the spread depends on — which is the only honest output available at this horizon.
Horizon
100 years is the economic frame; anything longer is a physics extrapolation

The two clocks underneath

Every version of this century depends on two rates that are measured elsewhere and assumed here. Longevity decides how many people are working in 2060 and how many are being carried; the price of a unit of machine capability decides how fast the automation curve arrives. Neither number appears anywhere above, which is a defect: a model whose answer moves with a parameter should show the parameter's evidence.
