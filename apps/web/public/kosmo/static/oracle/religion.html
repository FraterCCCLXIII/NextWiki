The demography and the practice figures are published. The research counts and pageviews are fetched. The trajectories are a model, and every lever below moves only the model — no measured chart changes when you drag one.

Three different objects, never mixed. This page asks how religious and spiritual culture develops, whether a post-integral share could become self-sustaining, and what that would take. It answers in three currencies. The demography is measured — Pew's 36,908-adult survey, 2,700 censuses, the General Social Survey. The practice is measured — how many adults meditate, how many papers mention mindfulness. The stages are not measured at all. No instrument scores a person as integral, no survey counts nondual awareness, and the words "metamodern" and "evolutionary" name claims rather than variables. Everything of that kind is drawn hollow or dashed, and the model that projects it is labelled wherever it appears.

Keeping the vessel, moving the centre

A tradition's cultural identity is not stored in its cosmology. It is stored in what its people do with their bodies on a schedule — the assembly, the burial, the fast, the chant, the festival, the pilgrimage, the language of prayer. Those things keep working after the cosmology is no longer believed, which is why "culturally Catholic" is a coherent sentence and "culturally Arian" is not. That distinction is the whole mechanism by which a religion can keep who it is while changing what it thinks the universe is.

Both axes here are asserted. No instrument scores a tradition's centre of gravity, and none scores how much of its membership is held by the carriers. What follows is an argument drawn as a diagram: the boundary is the claim, the shading is its consequence, and the three measured points on it are Pew's retention rates placed by hand. This is the weakest evidence on the page and the most practical section of it.

What an evolutionary cosmology would actually say

Not "the universe was created and the matter is settled", but "the universe has a history and is still under construction": matter, then life, then interiority, then culture, then a phase in which the process can notice itself and choose. The sacred is the process rather than a maker standing outside it; revelation is continuous rather than closed; and human development is not a fall from a golden age but a direction. This is not a new religion. It is a reading — Teilhard's noosphere, Whitehead's process, the evolutionary spirituality inside every contemplative school — and the traditions on this page that have a mystical wing already contain the materials for it. The ones that do not are the ones with a calendar and no interior.

Awakening inside that reading has three modes, and they are not interchangeable. Personal: the witness — awareness that persists across states, the "I am" behind the changing content, carried by devotion, confession, solitary prayer and retreat. Impersonal: emptiness — awareness with no subject at all, the nondual, carried only by transmission lineages, which is why the last page in this group can count 36 acharyas and 1,701 Chan masters and still not tell you how many people were awake. Collective: the shared field — a group whose attention becomes one thing, carried by assembly, chant, festival and service. The first is measured by practice surveys, the second only by recognition, and the third by nothing at all.

Six moves, each attached to a carrier

A transition that is argued doctrinally will fail, because doctrine is not what holds anyone. The moves that can work are all carrier moves.

  1. Re-read the canon, do not replace it. Read scripture as a first-person record of awakening — what a practitioner reported from the inside — rather than as physics or history. The text survives Darwin and Copernicus this way; it did not survive being read as a cosmology. This is the same move Augustine made with Genesis and the same one the Buddha's followers made with the Vedas.
  2. Keep the calendar and change what it commemorates. The year is the strongest carrier a tradition owns: it is public, it is repeated, it needs no belief to attend, and it is what makes a child belong before they can argue. A festival of the birth of a cosmos reads the same dates as a festival of the birth of a god.
  3. Keep the assembly, and say why. The collective mode is impossible without bodies in a room, and it is the mode with the weakest evidence and the highest ceiling. A tradition that keeps only private practice has kept the personal mode and abandoned the one it cannot replace.
  4. Ordain for competence, not assent. Transmission should pass what can be checked in a person — stability, ethical conduct, the capacity to sit and to teach — rather than agreement with a proposition. This is the only form of authority that an evolutionary cosmology can license, and it is what a lineage is for.
  5. Let the sacred language stay sacred. Liturgical languages survive as sound, rhythm and shared memory, not as claims about the world. The Latin, the Arabic, the Sanskrit or the Hebrew in the ear is identity; the same sentence as a factual assertion is a fight.
  6. Bury the dead, feed the poor, name the children. The carriers that carry collective awakening are the same ones that carry ordinary social life. A tradition that keeps its burial society and its soup kitchen keeps a congregation it will need for anything harder.

Who will do it, and what will resist

The move is led by the smallest group on this page — the integral and nondual share, roughly two to three per cent — and that group cannot staff an institution, which is the model's central constraint. The resistance comes from both directions at once. The modern stage finds the new cosmology plausible and drops the carriers, because the carriers look like superstition to it; it produces the unaffiliated at 73% retention and no grandchildren. The traditional stage keeps the carriers and refuses the centre, because moving the centre looks like surrender; it produces the tradition that still buries its dead and cannot speak to anyone under forty. Both are stable, and both lose. The carrier-strong, centre-moved quadrant is the only one that is both alive and transmissible, and it is the one nobody is staffing.

None of this predicts that any tradition will do it. What it does say is that the version of the transition people usually imagine — a new religion replacing the old, or belief quietly evaporating — is not how institutions change. They change by keeping the vessel and re-reading what is inside it, which is why the traditions with the deepest transmission lineages are the ones with the most to work with, and why the ones with the strongest calendar but no mystical wing are the ones most likely to fossilise into identity alone.

Could a critical mass form, and what would it take?

Five stages, four transmission channels, one threshold. This is a bookkeeping device, not a forecast: it has no regions, no war, no economy and no migration, and its initial shares are assumptions. What it can do is separate the levers — because the question "when does integral reach 25%" has a different answer depending on whether the bottleneck is persuasion, institutions, or the number of people who could sustain the stage at all.
