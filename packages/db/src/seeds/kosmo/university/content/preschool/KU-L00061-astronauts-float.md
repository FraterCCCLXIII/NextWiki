## Explanation

Let go of a ball. It falls down. Gravity pulls it.

Gravity pulls on you too. It keeps your feet on the ground.

Gravity pulls on everything. It pulls on a ball. It pulls on a cat. It pulls on the moon.

Astronauts ride a rocket up to space. Their spaceship goes around and around the Earth. In the spaceship, astronauts float. Their hair floats. Their pencil floats too.

Is gravity gone up there? No. Gravity is still pulling. The astronauts and the spaceship are both falling. They fall together, all the way around the Earth. So nothing pushes on their feet. That is why they float.

## Worked example

Think about a ride that drops down fast. For a moment, you feel light, like you are floating. You are falling. Astronauts feel that all the time. They fall around the Earth and never hit it.

## Activity

You need a soft toy or a small ball and an adult nearby.

1. Hold the toy up high. Ask, "What will happen?"
2. Let go over a soft rug or a bed. It falls. Say, "Gravity pulled it down."
3. Now take turns. Be the astronaut. Wave your arms slowly and pretend to float. Say, "Gravity is still pulling. We are falling together!"

## Summary

Gravity pulls everything down. Astronauts float because they and their spaceship fall around the Earth together. Gravity is not switched off.
