## Explanation

Gravity pulls on everything. It pulls on a big book. It pulls on a tiny feather.

Many people think heavy things fall faster. Let us test it. Drop a book and a feather. The book hits the floor first. Why?

The air is the reason. Air gets in the way. It pushes on things as they fall. A feather is wide and light, so the air slows it down a lot. A book is not slowed down much.

Now try a flat paper and a crumpled paper. They weigh the same. The flat one floats down slowly. The crumpled one drops fast. The only change is the shape. The air pushes less on the small ball.

What if there was no air? Then a heavy thing and a light thing would fall together. They would land at the same time. Astronauts have shown this on the Moon, where there is almost no air. A hammer and a feather landed together.

## Worked example

Hold a flat paper and a book at the same height. Drop them. The book wins.

Now put the paper on top of the book and drop both. The paper stays on the book all the way down. The book moves the air out of the way, so the paper falls with it. They fall together.

## Activity

You need two sheets of paper, a book, and an adult nearby.

1. Crumple one sheet into a ball. Keep the other flat.
2. Hold one in each hand at the same height. Count "1, 2, 3" and let go. Watch which lands first.
3. Draw what you saw.
4. Now try two balls of different size, such as a small ball and a bigger one of the same kind. Drop them together over a soft floor. Do they land together?

Stay on the floor or a low step. Do not climb. Drop the book over a rug, and keep toes out of the way.

## Summary

Gravity pulls on every object. Air slows down wide, light things, like a feather or a flat paper. Without air, heavy and light things fall together.
